## Per-assignment notes

### Non-negative blanks — tagged across all four sets (2026-08-10)
Every numeric part of `hw1`–`hw4` whose quantity cannot be negative carries `nonNegative: true`
in `_answerKeys.js` (73 of the 100 numeric items), so a negative entry gets a free nudge —
"This quantity can't be negative" — instead of a wrong verdict, and **no attempt is consumed**.
This matters most in the many "magnitude and direction" splits: a student who computes a
component and types the signed value into the magnitude blank has made a convention slip, not a
physics error, and the direction blank right beside it is where the sign belongs.

The 27 deliberately untagged items are the ones where a negative is *not* a slip:

| Kind | Items | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Vector components | `hw1_p2a`, `hw3_p1a_x/y`, `hw3_p2a_x/y`, `hw3_p5a_x/y`, `hw3_p5e_ax/ay/vx/vy` | the sign is the physics |
| Scalar product | `hw1_p6a` (−6.6 m²) | genuinely signed |
| Signed accelerations (2.16 a–c, "toward the left") | `hw2_p2a_a`, `hw2_p2b_a`, `hw2_p2c_a` | the sign is the answer |
| Angles / directions | all `*_d` items, `hw1_p9`, `hw3_p6a` | `angle: true` instead — see below |

### Angles accept coterminal answers (2026-08-10)
All 12 degree-valued items carry `angle: true` and grade with `angleMatch`, which folds whole
turns: **−19° now grades the same as 341°**, as do 701° and −379°. A student who reports a
direction as a small negative has named the identical direction the prompt's "degrees CCW from
+x" convention asks for, so it counts as correct rather than costing an attempt.

Only whole-turn shifts are folded, so genuinely different directions stay wrong — verified that
213.6° is still rejected for 33.6° (180° off), −33.6° is rejected for 33.6° (reflected, not
coterminal), and 236° is rejected for `hw1_p9`'s 124°. The tolerance and sig-fig leniency apply
to the shifted value exactly as they would to the original, so `-42.9` passes for 317.12°.

### ✅ hw3 — "Homework 3: Motion in Two Dimensions"
10 Ch. 3 problems (3.1, 3.6, 3.11, 3.15, 3.16, 3.22, 3.29, 3.51, 3.54, 3.63). Figures
`figE3-29.png` (Ferris wheel) / `figP3-63.png` (grasshopper cliff), copied straight from the
screenshots — no textbook caption to crop. Mostly deterministic numerics ($g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$):
vector/velocity questions split into component + magnitude + direction blanks (direction = degrees
CCW from +x, stated in the stem).

**3.6(c)** is an `answerType: "vector"` part — the student draws the two velocity arrows
$\vec v_1$, $\vec v_2$ (graded on direction + magnitude) **and the average-acceleration arrow**
$\vec a$ (graded on direction only — it must point along $\Delta\vec v$ at 31°, the misconception
being to draw it along $\vec v_2$) in `VectorField`, graded deterministically by `gradeVectors`.
The $\Delta\vec v$ vector is `freeTail`, so it can be drawn as the subtraction from $\vec v_1$'s
tip to $\vec v_2$'s tip **or** from the origin. Once the part resolves it auto-plays a
`VectorBuildup` illustration: ten $\bar a\,(1\text{ s})$ steps march tip-to-tail from $\vec v_1$'s
tip to $\vec v_2$'s tip while a running vector sweeps $\vec v_1\to\vec v_2$, driving home
$\vec v_2 = \vec v_1 + \bar a\,\Delta t$.

Claude is reserved for the remaining text parts and hints: **3.29(a)/(b)** acceleration directions
(toward the center — up at the bottom, down at the top) and **3.54(b)**.

⚠️ **3.54(b) is intentionally a `text` answer (ill-conditioned numerically).** With the *minimum*
muzzle velocity from 3.54(a), the trajectory's apex (~25.3 m) barely exceeds the 25.0-m cliff and
is reached *before* the edge (at x ≈ 54 m), so the shell is descending as it grazes the corner and
lands **essentially at the edge (~0 m beyond)**. The "distance past the edge" is hypersensitive to
how $v_0$ is rounded — it swings from negative (doesn't clear) to +2.6 m over
$v_0 = 32.6 \to 32.8\text{ m/s}$ — and the true value ≈ 0 has no meaningful ±2% tolerance band. So
it is graded as a conceptual `text` explanation rather than a fixed numeric.

### ✅ hw4 — "Homework 4: Newton's Laws of Motion"
10 Ch. 4 problems (4.4, 4.12, 4.19, 4.23, 4.27, 4.34, 4.37, 4.38, 4.40, 4.57). Figures
`figE4-4.png` (ramp) / `figE4-23.png` (boxes A,B) / `figP4-37.png` (cart forces) /
`figP4-38.png` (oil tanker) / `figP4-57.png` (boxes on a rope) — figure-only, no caption to crop.
Deterministic numerics use $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$, matching the source key (see the
2026-08-09 note under § Verification history).

**This set is the first use of the `FBDField` free-body-diagram builder** (`answerType: "fbd"`):

- **4.27** — two fbd parts, one per crate. The student draws forces from a bank ($F$/$T$/$N$/$w$),
  so the contact force shows up as a second normal ($N_1$ ground, $N_2$ contact) — plus `text`
  parts for the action–reaction pair and part (b).
- **4.34** — two fbd parts: (a) the box, where the **friction arrow is prefilled** (drawn & locked
  forward, $+x$, since friction is a HW5 topic) and the student adds $N$ and $w$; and (b) the
  truck, where **both friction forces are prefilled** (road traction forward $+x$, box's drag
  backward $-x$) and the student adds the truck's weight, the road's normal (up), and the box's
  normal pressing down on the bed. Then a (c) `text` part asks the student to identify the
  box↔bed action–reaction pairs across their two diagrams.
- **4.57** — scaffolded: (a) find the downward acceleration first, then two `fbd` parts —
  (b) box $A$ ($\vec F$ up, rope tension $T$ down, weight $w$ down; acceleration down) and
  (c) box $B$ ($T$ up, $w$ down; acceleration down), both with no normal force since the boxes
  hang on the rope — then (d) $m_B$, (e) $m_A$.

**The written action–reaction / FBD-description parts are graded with `diagramContext`** — the
runner feeds Claude a `describeFBDDiagram` summary of the FBD(s) the student actually drew (their
own $N_1$/$N_2$ labels and directions), so a student who labels the box-box contact force $N_1$ in
their diagram and answers "$N_1$" is graded against their own labeling, not a conventional default.
For 4.34 this context spans both FBDs.

Other text parts: 4.37(a) direction, 4.38(c) "will it hit / is the oil safe". 4.37 "magnitude and
direction" is split into a numeric magnitude + a `text` direction (the established pattern).
4.40(b) and 4.38(a) reveal in plain decimal (`toSigFigString` never uses sci-notation:
"3700000 N", "0.0022 m/s²"); students may type `3.7e6` (`parseNumber` accepts it).

⚠️ **4.38(b) is a mildly rounding-sensitive numeric (kept numeric).** The impact speed
$0.17\text{ m/s}$ comes from $v^2 = 2.25 - 2.222 = 0.028$, a small difference of larger numbers.
Carrying full precision keeps the canonical 0.17 inside the ±2% band, so it stays numeric (unlike
3.54(b), whose value ≈ 0 had no meaningful tolerance band).
